    The spine — memorize this before any feature

    member → documented work → evidence → money or proof

    Everything in the platform is that one chain, viewed from a different altitude by a different person. If you can say the chain, you can explain any screen.

    AltitudeWhoThe question it answers
    1 · MemberNavigatorWho needs me right now, and what do I do for this person today?
    2 · Open monthSupervisorIs the book of business on track — what can still be fixed?
    3 · Closed monthSupervisor / practiceWhat did we document, what is supportable, what was held?
    4 · ProgramExternal partnerIs the program working — without exposing anyone's patients?
    Tour — the surfaces worth knowing, and the one idea behind each

    Worklist — ranks the navigator's day by which member-months can still be saved. Safety alerts are pinned above revenue, always — a 12-minute shortfall can never outrank an unacknowledged crisis alert. That ordering is enforced by tests, not policy.

    Capture — field-fast contact logging. Site and funding source stay sticky between saves (re-picking 30× an afternoon is how capture stops happening); identity is optional at capture, because you can't demand an ID before a blood-pressure check.

    Member → patient month — the evidence view: minutes, activities, consent, initiating-visit status, per code family, per month. This is what "we prove the work happened" looks like on screen.

    Revenue-at-risk vs Billing — same subject, two surfaces on purpose. Revenue-at-risk is the open month (what can still be fixed); Billing is the closed month (what settled). Different questions, different people, deliberately not merged.

    The billing packet — supported lines vs held lines, each held line with its reason (below threshold, no consent, awaiting review, no initiating visit). Immutable artifact with a hash; their biller records line dispositions; their coder decides the claim. Holding lines back is the feature — it's what makes a compliance officer a fan.

    Health Home pack (NY) — a different payment model: a month clears a contact count or silently downgrades (downstate: $925 → $387, a 58% loss from documentation alone). The pack reports at-risk as the delta, not the full rate — overstating exposure gets you dismissed by anyone who knows their rates.

    Outreach & events — the church-event → roster → attribution path. Follow-up ranks consented-then-stalled people first: consent is the expensive step and it's already paid for.

    Funder report — grant-facing impact cut with banded demographics and small-cell suppression (minimum cell 11, all-or-nothing) so no report can identify a person by subtraction.

    Network — cross-org roll-up for a lead entity, aggregate-only. No drill-through exists by design; adding one is a legal boundary, not a UI change.

    Integration planes (EHR · claims · safety delivery) — rehearsal-ready seams, everything fail-closed behind hard-off flags. A route existing is not a vendor being activated — activation is a per-buyer decision with signed gates.

    Integration ops AI — reads receipts, summarizes, proposes next steps for a human. It cannot choose a code, match a patient, send anything, or write anywhere. Every AI in the product is advisory and evidence-linked; that's a sales point, not a limitation.

    CEO: "New, additive Medicare revenue for care work your team already does — with evidence that survives an audit."

    CFO: "A flat fee that never moves with your billing, your own panel's arithmetic, and a 120-day walk-away if we don't prove it."

    Quality officer: "A quality officer's job is to never let anything fall through the cracks. That's the whole product: every member-month either carries its evidence or tells you exactly why it's held." (Dr. Silber's framing — lead with this for clinical-quality audiences.)

    Compliance officer: "Ask what you'd want a vendor to refuse to do — that's our feature list. We never choose a code, the packet holds lines back, the audit trail is append-only."

    RCM / billing director: "We replace exactly one input to your existing process — the assembly step — and it arrives audit-ready. The claim stays yours."

    A navigator (end user): "It tells you who needs you today, and turns the work you already do into credit for it."

    Your family at dinner: "Health centers do a lot of care work Medicare would pay for, but they can't prove it well enough to bill it. We're the proof."

    What we never say — the boundary list
    • We never say "we bill" or "we submit claims" — they bill; we prove the work happened.
    • We never call it an EHR or a care-delivery tool — it's the evidence ledger beside their EHR.
    • We never say "we increase your billing" — we evidence work already done. (Recovered revenue, not generated revenue.)
    • We never guarantee reimbursement or audit outcomes — "we make you auditable," never "we make you compliant."
    • We never quote $117.24 without the QMB discount — the modeled number is $93.79.
    • We never cite caseload ratios (unconfirmed in primary sources) and never take a percentage of collections — the refusal is the sales asset.
    • We never claim AI decides anything clinical or financial — it's advisory, evidence-linked, read-only.
    • We never claim a vendor/EHR/clearinghouse integration is live — seams exist; activation is gated per buyer.
    Honest FAQ — quick answers for anyone who asks

    Is it live? Yes — keptcount.com, demo-ready on synthetic data, 1,000+ automated tests. The covered (real-data) environment is provisioned when a pilot signs, behind signed BAAs.

    Is there real patient data in it? No. Everything today is synthetic by design. Real PHI only ever enters a dedicated HIPAA environment that doesn't exist until a pilot signs.

    Does it text patients? Not in founding scope — deferred deliberately (carrier registration requires the incorporated entity, and inbound crisis replies create a duty to respond that must be staffed first). The ledger records escalation handoffs to the practice's own protocols.

    Does it replace our EHR / billing system? No. It sits beside them: roster in via CSV or FHIR, evidence packet out to the existing billing flow. Nothing about their claim submission changes.

    Who's behind it? Kept Count, Inc. (in formation) — founder Lorenzo Daughtry-Chambers. Deliberately small: three founding health centers, onboarded properly rather than quickly.

    What does it cost? Founding terms: $15–25K implementation, $2,500/mo for a 120-day proof with defined acceptance criteria and a walk-away, then $22 per active managed patient/mo ($5K minimum, rate locked 24 months). Never a percentage of collections.

    How the codes work — the two lanes

    Meet Ms. Rivera: 71, diabetes + hypertension, dual-eligible (QMB), established patient for years. She's the example for everything below.

    Lane 1 · APCM — the "no stopwatch" lane

    A flat monthly payment per enrolled patient for having and delivering care management — a subscription per patient. Nobody counts minutes.

    LevelCodeWho qualifiesCY2026 / mo
    L1G05561 chronic condition$16.37
    L2G05572+ chronic conditions$53.78
    L3G05582+ chronic AND QMB dual$117.24 → model $93.79

    Ms. Rivera is L3 — billable every month, no minute threshold. The $93.79: QMB patients legally can't be charged the 20% coinsurance, so the center collects Medicare's 80%. Quote $117.24 without the discount and a sharp CFO writes you off.

    APCM's requirements are capability + consent, not minutes: practice-level care-management capability (24/7 access to the care team — an FQHC's existing after-hours line satisfies it), one-time patient consent, and an initiating visit only for NEW patients. Established patients like Ms. Rivera: consent, enroll, deliver, bill monthly. This is why APCM is the fastest lane.

    Lane 2 · CHI and PIN — the "stopwatch" lane

    CHI (G0019, $86.17): a CHW addressing the upstream drivers that interfere with treatment — housing, food, transport, smoking. First 60 documented minutes in a month; each extra 30 min (G0022) $54.11.
    PIN (G0023, $87.18): navigation for one serious high-risk condition — cancer, serious mental illness. Same shape; +30 min (G0024) $54.44.

    The brutal math: 59 documented minutes = $0. 60 minutes = $86.17. A month clears the threshold or it's worth nothing — which is exactly why an evidence ledger exists.

    The gate: both CHI and PIN require a billing practitioner's initiating visit before any time counts (once per episode). A CHW meeting someone at a church event cannot start the clock. Knowing this is what makes you sound like an operator.

    "Their number" — the arithmetic

    The anchor: one enrolled L3 dual ≈ $1,125/year ($93.79 × 12 = $1,125.48, exactly).

    1 · Pull their public UDS data → dual-eligible count.
    2 · FQHC duals almost always carry 2+ chronic conditions → L3 candidates.
    3 · Their number = duals × $1,125/yr. Never claim full enrollment — APCM is voluntary per patient. Scale honestly: “even enrolling ten percent of your duals is $X — additive to your PPS payments.” Understate and let them scale it up.

    Build the number on APCM only — never CHI/PIN. A CHI/PIN forecast depends on attend rates and initiating-visit flow you don't have data for; projecting it is “a guess dressed as a forecast.” Say that out loud — it's another discount-your-own-number trust move.

    The two sentences that hold it all: “APCM is a monthly per-patient payment for managing your complex patients — no stopwatch, and your established duals are the top tier. CHI and PIN pay for documented time — sixty minutes clears the bar, fifty-nine is zero, and a practitioner has to start the clock.”
    Why the scope excludes what it excludes — the moat

    Every exclusion protects one of two boundaries: clinical authority and billing authority. Cross either and Kept Count stops being a software vendor and becomes a participant in care delivery or federal claims.

    Coding: if the software chooses codes and the fee comes from billing-dependent customers, that's the AKS shape. “Our fee does not move with your billing” is only true because we never select a code. We qualify evidence against published requirements — supported vs held, with reasons — and their coder decides the claim.

    Claim submission: billing-capable centers already own clearinghouse pipes. A vendor touching claims inherits every denial. The packet drops into their existing flow unchanged.

    Messaging & crisis delivery: texting patients means receiving “I want to die” at 2am — a duty to respond a software vendor cannot staff. Their clinicians keep crisis authority; the ledger records the escalation and handoff. (Also: A2P registration binds to an EIN and takes carrier weeks — physically impossible to promise inside a 45-day go-live.)

    Automated screening: deciding who's at risk is clinical judgment — FDA/malpractice territory. Our AI is read-only and evidence-linked; it summarizes and proposes, never decides.

    The reframe: the boundaries are enforced in the product, not promised in a contract. When counsel asks “what's your exposure to our billing?” the answer is “structurally none — here's the schema that can't express it.” No percentage-of-collections competitor can say that.
    Pricing — why a paid pilot, and the levers

    Free pilots in health tech have a name: “pilot purgatory.” Nobody paid → nobody owns it. Free skips procurement → conversion must start procurement from zero. Free signals unproven. Paid pilots are the modern norm: $25K–$100K in health-system land; implementation fees of 50–100% of annual subscription are ordinary for system-of-record software.

    Our structure sits inside the band: $15–25K implementation (founder-led services, not just a login) + $2,500 × 4 months, with the 120-day defined-criteria walk-away — a de-risk most vendors won't offer.

    The check that matters: they compare the pilot to their number, not to software budgets. A CFO who won't pay $25K to recover $200K doesn't have a price objection — they don't believe the number yet. Fix that with the demo, not a discount.

    Levers (structure, never rate): 1· split implementation 50/50 signature/go-live — keeps the decision inside CFO authority · 2· start at $15K with a 50-patient first cohort · 3· credit the $10K of launch fees against year one on acceptance. Never drop the implementation fee — unpaid implementation is unstaffed implementation. Never a percentage of collections. Never discount the rate: the first price you accept becomes your price forever.

    OIG & compliance — verified Aug 2026

    The facts: OIG added CCM billing to its active Work Plan in March 2026 — auditing Medicare payments where patients may not meet the 2+ qualifying chronic conditions requirement, expected through FY2028. OIG's General Compliance Program Guidance (Nov 2023) defines seven program elements; monitoring and auditing is one — the packet is direct evidence for it. Analysts note the 2026 OIG priorities share one root cause: insufficient documentation.

    The pitch line (after the teach, before their number): “OIG put care-management billing on its active Work Plan this spring — they're auditing whether patients actually meet the chronic-condition requirements behind these codes, through FY2028. So the question isn't just 'can we bill this' — it's 'can we bill it and be the center that passes that audit.' That's the ledger's job: it counts distinct conditions once — conservatively — so a member can't be over-tiered, and every billed month carries its evidence.”

    For the compliance officer: fee independence · we never select a code · held lines with reasons · append-only audit trail · conservative condition counting prevents the exact over-tiering failure OIG is auditing for. Offer LEIE exclusion screening before they ask.

    The boundary: “we make you auditable” — never “we make you compliant.” Kept Count is evidence infrastructure their compliance program uses; it is not a compliance program, counsel, or a guarantee of audit outcomes.

    How it plugs into their RCM — the handoff

    1 · Monthly packet lands: supported vs held lines per member-month, immutable artifact with a hash, evidence attached.
    2 · Their biller reviews — line dispositions recorded — their coder finalizes codes in their PM system.
    3 · Claims go out through their clearinghouse exactly as today. Same submitter, same payers, same posting, same collections.
    4 · Denials stay theirs — but when a denial questions the work, the ledger produces the month's evidence. Corrections return through a recorded lineage.

    The only thing that changes in their world: the assembly step. The pitch to a skeptical RCM director: “zero workflow disruption — we replace one input to your existing process, and it arrives audit-ready.” Their RCM lead signing off on this flow is a formal activation gate — and it's the working-session meeting.
