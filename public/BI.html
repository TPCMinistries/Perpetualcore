    The arc of the call

    Roughly 40 minutes. The ratio that wins: listen more than you talk. Bryant asked to learn about IHA — but what makes him an advocate inside the firm is feeling heard about what they're building.

    1 Open — 2 minutes

    Thank him for the time; credit Jessica warmly. Restate the no-ask posture from the cover email in one breath:

    "I meant what I wrote — I'm not coming with an ask. I'd like to understand what you're building and see whether our maps overlap."

    Then hand him the floor first if he'll take it. If he says "tell me about IHA" — that's your opener drill. 90 seconds, then back to him.

    2 His turn — get him talking (10–15 min)

    Questions that show you did the reading and give you the overlap material:

    • "What does External Affairs own at BlackIvy — where do you sit between the portfolio and the outside world?"
    • "Where do the portfolio companies feel talent constraints most — clinical, technical, managerial?"
    • "When you stand up something like LuccaHealth or BigCold in a new community, how do you approach local adoption and trust?"
    • "What does the next 18 months look like across Accra, Nairobi, Dar?"
    • "I read the Lions to Unicorns conversation — is that still how the firm frames the moment?" (signals homework, invites thesis talk)

    Every answer he gives is a hook for your overlap map in part 4. Take notes visibly.

    3 Your turn — the story in 3 moves (10 min)

    Move 1 — the institution and the engine. 501(c)(3) platform institution, Aspen/Brookings model, with the Perpetual Engine underneath: Educate → Deploy → Capitalize → Scale. Three kinds of capital, one structure. "This isn't charity. It's architecture."

    Move 2 — Kenya, because you ran it. The numbers, delivered cold (drill tab). Five-partner coalition, UNDP included. Left IHA Advance in-country. "Not a study. A deployment."

    Move 3 — the part that can't be manufactured. The 350+ pastors and leaders. The network was already there — its leaders invited TPC in to steward the work. "A network you build takes years; a network that invites you in is standing trust, already earned." Then the thesis: in these markets the binding constraint is rarely capital or product — it's trust and last-mile adoption.

    If it lands, he'll make the connection to their portfolio himself. Let him say it first if he's close.

    4 Map the overlap — together (10 min)

    Walk the portfolio table (Room tab) conversationally, anchored to whatever he said in part 2. The frame:

    "Trained talent and community trust de-risk an operating business. That's not philanthropy adjacent to your portfolio — it's an input to it. And it doesn't have to be an expense — this is the kind of work that can be structured as a jointly built asset."

    Only if he asks "what could this look like" — the three shapes, as shapes, not a menu: pilot (health + workforce around one venture; Kenya natural first) · co-build (a jointly created workforce company — an asset, not an expense) · co-invest (later, alongside capital partners and DeepFutures, in formation).

    5 Close — 2 minutes, no ask
    "This confirmed what I hoped — we're working the same countries from different layers of the stack. I'd love to keep the maps side by side. What would be useful on your end — a working session with whoever's closest to talent on the portfolio side, or should the two of us go another round first?"

    A next step with a name and a date beats any amount of enthusiasm. If he offers to "share it internally," offer to tailor one page for whoever he's sharing it with — that creates the second touch.
