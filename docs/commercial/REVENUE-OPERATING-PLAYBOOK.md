# Perpetual Core Revenue Operating Playbook

**Owner:** Perpetual Core LLC  
**Mode:** Founder-led, evidence-first, no-send until explicitly approved  
**Canonical offers:** Operating System Map, Workflow Proof Sprint, Managed Operating Lane

## 1. Revenue-system architecture

Revenue Studio is Perpetual Core's durable commercial workspace. It holds the
Business Brain, offer lineage, prospect research, approvals, revenue evidence,
and delivery proof. The public website is the acquisition and intake surface.
Sage may later display bounded aggregate status, but it must not become a copy
of prospect PII, message content, provider credentials, or payment records.

| Layer | Authority | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Public website | Explain, qualify, and capture buyer intent | Page/event receipt and durable intake receipt |
| Revenue Studio | Company truth, offers, research, approvals, evidence | Workspace, version, hash, source, and audit receipts |
| Sales intake tables | Store the buyer-submitted inquiry | Successful write and exact offer/source labels |
| Sage executive view | Future aggregate visibility only | Bounded counts, stage totals, timestamps, and source receipt |
| Accounting/payment provider | Settled revenue authority | Verified settled payment receipt |

## 2. Ideal customer profile

### Primary ICP

Knowledge-intensive, people-heavy organizations where a recurring workflow
crosses multiple teams, tools, or locations and the cost of lost context is
material. Best initial range: approximately 25–1,000 employees or a funded
program/business unit with a named executive sponsor and operating owner.

### Likely buyers

- Founder, CEO, COO, executive director, or operating partner
- Functional leader responsible for opportunity, knowledge, care, people, or diligence
- Program leader with budget, workflow authority, and a measurable mandate
- Technical or risk reviewer who needs boundaries, provenance, and human control

### Trigger conditions

- A costly handoff repeatedly fails between teams or systems
- Leadership is evaluating AI but cannot identify a safe first production use
- A pilot produced activity but no durable operating change or proof standard
- Reporting, follow-up, or institutional memory depends on one overloaded person
- A new contract, program, portfolio, or growth phase creates coordination pressure

### Disqualifiers

- No executive sponsor, workflow owner, or budget path
- Buyer wants generic AI training without an operating use case
- Buyer requires guaranteed outcomes controlled by adoption or third parties
- Buyer expects autonomous consequential action without human authority
- Required data, legal, security, or procurement gates cannot be defined
- The problem is too small to justify the proposed economics

## 3. Offer ladder and qualification

| Offer | Best entry condition | Commercial hypothesis | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Operating System Map | Problem is material but first workflow or economics are unclear | $7.5K–$15K; 1–2 weeks | Prioritized workflow, baseline, authority map, implementation range, 90-day decision sequence |
| Workflow Proof Sprint | Workflow, owner, baseline, and production boundary can be defined | $30K–$75K; 4–8 weeks | Working bounded workflow, training/runbook, launch receipts, outcome-review plan |
| Managed Operating Lane | Proven or strategically required workflow needs ongoing ownership | $10K–$35K/month; 90-day initial term | Monthly queue, evidence/exception report, operating review, explicit renew/expand/stop decision |

### Routing rule

Sell the smallest offer capable of resolving the buyer's next uncertainty. Do
not force a Sprint when the workflow is still ambiguous. Do not sell a Managed
Lane when ownership, capacity, or evaluation criteria are undefined.

## 4. Funnel stages and evidence gates

| Stage | Definition | Evidence required to enter | Required next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Buyer submitted or directly requested a conversation | Durable intake record with identity, organization, offer/source, and timestamp | Human review within one business day |
| Triaged | Basic fit and urgency reviewed | ICP fit note, disqualifier check, selected offer hypothesis | Accept for discovery, nurture, refer, or close |
| Discovery scheduled | Buyer accepted a scoped conversation | Calendar receipt and named attendees | Prepare workflow hypothesis and questions |
| Qualified | Problem, sponsor, owner, economics, timeline, and gates are credible | Completed qualification scorecard and evidence gaps | Map/Sprint/Lane recommendation |
| Solution aligned | Buyer agrees on proposed result and evaluation standard | Written scope outline, success measures, authority and data boundaries | Prepare proposal and SOW |
| Proposal | Commercial terms issued | Exact proposal/SOW version and delivery receipt | Named follow-up date and procurement owner |
| Verbal alignment | Buyer indicates intent subject to contracting | Decision note identifying remaining legal/procurement/payment gates | Resolve gates without marking won |
| Contracted | Authorized signatures complete | Executed agreement and SOW | Invoice or approved payment path |
| Paid kickoff | Required payment has settled | Provider-verified settled receipt | Delivery handoff and kickoff |
| Active delivery | Work has begun under signed scope | Kickoff receipt, owner, plan, risk register | Weekly evidence and exception review |
| Outcome review | Agreed evaluation window reached | Baseline versus observed evidence, limitations, buyer acceptance status | Expand, renew, revise, or stop |
| Won | Contracted and payment requirement satisfied | Executed scope plus required settled receipt | Never infer from verbal interest |
| Lost/paused | Buyer declined, delayed, or failed a gate | Reason and re-entry condition | Close respectfully; no invented urgency |

## 5. Qualification scorecard

Score each dimension 0–2. A score of **9+ of 12**, with no zero in Sponsor,
Owner, or Authority, is the working threshold for a proposal.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consequence | Vague inconvenience | Recurring measurable friction | Material revenue, cost, quality, risk, or mission consequence |
| Sponsor | No sponsor | Interested leader | Executive sponsor with decision influence |
| Owner | Nobody owns workflow | Partial owner | Named operational owner with time and accountability |
| Evidence | No baseline | Directional evidence | Credible baseline and observable success measure |
| Authority/data | Unknown or unacceptable | Gates identifiable | Boundaries and approval path can be defined |
| Budget/timing | No path | Possible future path | Credible budget mechanism and decision window |

## 6. Thirty-minute discovery call

### 0–3 minutes — frame

“This is not a generic AI demo. I want to understand the recurring work, where
it breaks, who owns it, and what evidence would justify—or reject—an install.”

### 3–12 minutes — workflow

1. What recurring result must move from trigger to completion?
2. Where does context change hands, disappear, or require reconstruction?
3. Which people, systems, inboxes, documents, and approvals are involved?
4. How often does this occur and what happens when it fails or slows down?

### 12–20 minutes — authority and evidence

5. Who owns the workflow and who can approve a change?
6. Which system remains authoritative for each critical record?
7. What actions must remain human-approved?
8. What baseline exists today, and what observable measure would justify expansion?

### 20–26 minutes — buying path

9. Why is this a priority now?
10. Who else reviews operations, technology, risk, legal, and procurement?
11. Is there a budget path and a target decision date?

### 26–30 minutes — recommendation

Recommend one of four paths: Operating System Map, Workflow Proof Sprint,
Managed Operating Lane, or no engagement. State the missing evidence and exact
next step. Do not promise a proposal when qualification is incomplete.

## 7. Proposal standard

Every proposal and SOW must include:

1. Buyer situation and named workflow
2. Current consequence and available baseline
3. Deliverable and explicit exclusions
4. Roles, customer dependencies, and system-of-record boundaries
5. Human approval and escalation rules
6. Success measures and evidence plan
7. Timeline, milestones, acceptance criteria, and change control
8. Fees, payment schedule, expenses, and taxes
9. Security, privacy, AI, and legal document references
10. Named decision date and next action

## 8. Founder follow-up cadence

These are drafts only until the user approves the exact recipient and message.

| Timing | Purpose | Core message |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Confirm understanding | Restate the workflow, consequence, owner, and one unresolved question |
| +2 business days | Deliver useful clarity | Send the agreed scope outline, evidence gap, or Map recommendation |
| +5 business days | Decision support | Ask whether the proposed result and evaluation standard are correct |
| +10 business days | Resolve the gate | Name the apparent budget, authority, legal, or timing blocker without pressure |
| +20 business days | Close or re-date | Offer a clear pause and ask what event should trigger re-entry |

### Same-day draft

Subject: The workflow I heard

Thank you for the conversation. I heard one central operating problem: **[named
workflow]** is losing **[context/time/revenue/quality/visibility]** between
**[handoff points]**. **[owner]** owns the result, while **[authority boundary]**
must remain explicit. The evidence that would justify the next investment is
**[measure]**. I will use that—not a generic AI promise—as the basis for the
recommended scope. The one point I still need to resolve is **[question]**.

### Proposal follow-up draft

Subject: Decision on [Map/Sprint/Lane]

The proposal is designed to answer one decision: whether **[workflow result]**
can improve under the agreed authority and evidence boundaries. Is the result,
scope, and evaluation standard correct? If yes, the remaining gate appears to
be **[procurement/legal/budget/timing]**. If that is not accurate, tell me what
I am missing and I will correct it directly.

## 9. Weekly founder revenue review

Run once each week using receipts rather than memory.

1. New durable inquiries and their source/offer labels
2. Human response-time exceptions
3. Qualified opportunities by offer and next action
4. Proposals without a named decision date
5. Contract, procurement, and payment gates
6. Delivery evidence and expansion decisions
7. Pipeline value as a hypothesis; settled revenue separately
8. One bottleneck to remove during the coming week

## 10. Critical implementation boundary

Do not connect Revenue Studio and Sage by copying raw leads. The first safe
integration, when separately authorized, is a one-way aggregate receipt such as:

- workspace ID and reporting date
- inquiry/qualified/proposal/contracted counts
- aggregate hypothesized pipeline value by offer
- verified settled revenue total from authoritative receipts
- stale-opportunity and response-time exception counts
- source system, generation timestamp, and reconciliation status

No prospect names, email addresses, message bodies, proposal text, provider
tokens, payment data, or protected records cross that boundary.
